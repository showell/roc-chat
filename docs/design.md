# roc-chat: the design from first principles

The answers from our listening session on 2026-11-02, and Steve's calls since, gathered in one place for you to correct. Where you gave words, these are your words. A rule marked with a name and a date is that person's call; a rule with none is ours, written without you, for your review. Anything marked **open** we haven't settled yet.

## Who it is for

- **A small group of humans and their agents.** Agents do real work and report back where everyone can see it.
- **An agent's report lands in the conversation where the work was asked for.**
- An agent mostly works for its owner, but anyone can ask it things. For now, assume Steve is the only person who works with agents.

## The nouns

### Message

- **A message is Author + DateTime + MarkdownContent, plus the id its conversation gives it.**
- A **MessagePayload** is Author + MarkdownContent, as the writer sent it. With its DateTime it is an unnumbered message (`Message.Unnumbered`): every part checked, waiting only for the Id its conversation gives it. The full Message, with its Id and DateTime, is made as early as possible.
- **Content is markdown text, and may include images.** The flavour is whatever our markdown implementation accepts. Images are opaque to a message: a message with an image tag is valid even when the image can't be reached.
- **Messages are immutable.** An edit is a new message that edits an earlier one: its markdown starts `Edit of MSG_<topic>_<n>`, as Gopher Chat's did, and only the message's author may post it (`app/Edit.roc`). When rendered, the original says "Edited in" the edit, its own markdown folded away; the stream sends it again, drawn so, to every open page.
- **A message doesn't care about eligibility.** "Steve said hello on Tuesday" is a message. Whether Steve may say that in a given place is up to the conversation, not the message.

### Author and User

- **A User is a human or an agent whose identity has already been checked.** Identity is checked at the edge of the system. Messier things like `UnauthenticatedUser` stay at that edge, and everything inside deals in Users.
- **An Author is a User in the role of writing a message.** Pass around small views such as `AuthorInfo` (name and id). A conversation never sees a user's GitHub login, last sign-in or preferences.
- **Names are permanent and case-sensitive.** Someone who signs up as "Steve Howell" is never called "steve howell" by the system. Names are unique regardless of case, so nobody else can be "sTeVe hoWEll". Inside the system, a form like `steve-howell-1` is fine.
- **Every user also has a unique id, and Steve is always id 1.** Ids are handy in URLs. Wherever the name and the id both appear, the system checks that they match.
- **Humans, agents and channels share one namespace.**

### Conversation

- **A conversation is a series of messages written by authors eligible to take part in it.**
- **Ids are sequential within a conversation, so a conversation's order is its id order.**
- **Times are UTC and never go backwards within a conversation.** A message's time is never earlier than the latest message's in its conversation. Equal times are allowed, because times are to the second and two messages can share one. Localising times for display can make two shown times look out of order once a year, when the clocks go back (in the US on 2026-11-01, 1:59 a.m. was followed by 1:00 a.m.), and that is expected.
- **A conversation can stand alone.** You can hand one over and get a RenderedConversation back, without knowing whether it's a channel's or a DM's. That is also how it gets tested.
- **Every conversation has a topic name, which is mandatory.** It also has a unique name on disk and a unique URL for those allowed to read it. Clients, and sometimes the server, cut the friction of naming a topic by suggesting "general" or "ChitChat".
- **A topic name is unique only within its channel or DM.**
- **Long conversations:** a reader gets the whole conversation straight away, with no paging. We don't cater for long conversations; we make them easy to archive or turn into docs. They tend to die off on their own, and Steve only steps in on topics like "general".

### Channel and DM

- **A channel is a named set of members, plus its topics (its conversations).** It may later track more, such as when it was made.
- **A DM is a channel whose members are exactly one or two humans.** One human is your own DM with yourself.
- **DMs are for humans only, for now.** Agents take part only in channels. A channel holding just an agent and its owner is still a named channel.
- **Only an admin creates a channel and chooses its members, for now, by their own session and never by an API key.** This is very likely to change. The one exception: a new human's account starts in the default channel, `general` (`Channels.default_name`), which the first start makes (Steve, 2026-11-05). The admin may remove them from it as from any channel.
- **A DM is named by its pair of user ids, in numeric order:** `7_11`, never `11_7`, and one human alone is `5_5`, as in the previous app. A channel's name starts with a letter and holds no `_`, so the two never collide.
- **A DM begins with its first message.** A new human's first DM begins as their account is made: the owner's welcome (below).
- **A new human is welcomed in a DM with the chat's owner (Steve, 2026-11-05).** When a human's account is made, by an invite or by GitHub sign-up, the DM between them and the owner starts under the topic `ChitChat`, with one message from the owner: "Welcome! If Steve is not here, you can still send a message, and they will reply soon.", the name looked up (`app/Welcome.roc`). It goes in the transcript like any other. The DM is named by ids and the message names only the owner, so an invited human's new name changes neither.

### Reaction

- **Reactions are not messages; they are their own thing**, drawn beneath the message they belong to.
- **A reaction is one author, one emoji and one message.** A message has zero or more reactions, and zero is the usual case. Two authors can react with the same emoji, and one author can react with several emojis. An author can't react twice with the same emoji, and clicking it again removes it.
- **No history:** only the current set is kept.
- Agents never react, but there's no need to enforce that.

### Images

- **An uploaded image belongs to one conversation**, and exactly that conversation's readers can see it.
- **The HTML gives an image's dimensions** in the native HTML way.

### Admin

- **Steve is the only admin for now.** There may be more later, but always all-or-nothing: no levels of admin inside the chat.
- **The chat's owner is the first human and the admin.** `UserId.chat_owner` is the id's one home and `Directory.chat_owner` the human's, whose name is read from their file.

### Agent

- **Every agent has exactly one owner, always a human.** The owner creates the agent in the chat and gives it a key.
- **Only the admin works with agents, for now.** Making an agent, inviting it, and listing or revoking its keys and invites are refused (a 403) to every other human, as sign-up is open to anyone with GitHub (`Agents.agent_keeper`). Every human still reaches `/agents`, which offers them the button that links GitHub.
- Nothing proves an agent is an LLM: a Python bot holding a key would look the same. We have no use for old-style chatbots.
- **Agents use API keys**, so they never drive a browser or a complicated HTTP exchange. They can read the same HTML humans see. Raw markdown with author, id and timestamp as JSON is possible, but no agent has asked for it yet.
- **No mentions as a mechanism.** An agent listens to every message in its channels and works out what applies to it, while also understanding the `@CC` convention.
- **No hop limit.** Agents are not prone to replying to each other forever. Eventually there will be one general rate limit, the same for humans and agents, against abuse.
- **How an agent gets its key.** Its owner, signed in, makes it at `/agents` and invites it. The invite's one-time code is shown once, lives ten minutes and redeems once: the agent posts `code=<code>` to `/agents/redeem` and is answered with its key on one line of text. The key is the agent's id, `-`, and 64 hex digits (32 random bytes from the host), sent as `Authorization: Bearer <key>`, as the Zig chat's keys were. Only its hash is kept. A key lives a year, its owner can list and revoke it at `/agents`, and its scope can turn off its reading or its posting. A key never makes an agent, invites, redeems, or lists or revokes keys.
- **What an agent does with its key.** It posts with the same form a browser posts (`markdown=`, to a conversation's URL, a 303; or to its `/send`, a 201 with the message's HTML, or a refusal in one line of text). It reads a conversation's transcript as plain text at `<conversation>/raw`. It listens at `/agents/stream`: one connection carrying every message of every channel it is a member of, each event one line of JSON (`conversation`, `id`, `from`, `from_id`, the author's user id, `date`, `markdown`); with `?since=<date>` it first sends everything since that date.
- **Who may read and post where (for your review).** A channel's members, humans and agents alike, read and post in each of its conversations; a DM's one or two humans in theirs; nobody else, the admin included, until the admin adds themself.

## Delivery

- **When a message lands, every member of the channel who is signed in gets it straight away.** There are no notifications and no tracking of unread messages.
- **Recent** has one tile per conversation, newest first, by each conversation's latest message, as Gopher Chat's `/chat/recent` had (`app/RecentFeed.roc`): every conversation the viewer may read but their own DM, which Gopher's left out too; each tile names its place and topic, how long ago its last message was sent, and who sent it with an excerpt, which reads as text, markdown's marks taken out (`MarkdownMarks`). A conversation with no message yet has no tile. On a phone a tile is chat-client's, a DM shown by its other human's initial; a wide window lets the page breathe (Steve, 2026-11-20: "There is no reason I should have to read "A" to see that it's a DM from Apoorva"): each tile is a row of columns, its place spelled out ("#general" with the channel's colour, "DM with Apoorva" with their presence dot), the topic over two lines of excerpt, and the time on the right. A tile that arrives live glows for a moment, and the row under the pointer stays put. There is no unread state to mark, as above. Each tile's last message is read from its transcript's last record alone (`Transcript.latest_message`), so the page reads each transcript but decodes one message of it. It is live, as Gopher's was: each Recent page holds a stream on its viewer's own key (`/recent/<id>`, `RecentStream`), and each message added is sent as its tile to every human who may read it, the page putting it first in place of the conversation's old one.
- **A live message arrives as the same HTML a full reload would show.**
- **If a browser's connection drops, it catches up as the Zig chat's did:** the browser reconnects by itself and the server sends only the messages it missed. If the stream dies for good, the page reloads itself; if it dies again within a minute, the page shows a notice instead (Steve, 2026-11-03).
- **An agent holds one connection carrying all of its channels.** If that's awkward to build, that's a design smell. When the connection drops, the agent catches up by asking for **everything since a timestamp** (message ids would also work).
- How a browser's connections are split is my call, checked against what the Zig chat does. New messages matter more than, say, presence, and the two can run at different rates.
- **Presence, as the Zig chat's (Steve, 2026-11-19: "the first thing he missed", of Apoorva).** A human is online for five minutes after any request of theirs that is not a stream; a stream is the browser's work. The request that ends five minutes of quiet, or a human's first since the server started, tells every other human, on the stream their conversation page already holds: their sidebar's dot for that DM fills, and the top bar's notice line says "<name> has come online.", linked to their DM with them (its first topic, or `general`). No one is told when a human goes quiet: their dot goes hollow on the next page drawn, as in the Zig chat. **The viewer has a dot too, always green**, wherever another human's would be: their own DM in the sidebar, their own row among a channel card's members, and their own card, since they are making the very request that draws it (Steve, 2026-11-20: "Show the presence bubble for Steve to Steve. This is how we teach people how presence works! You see green for Steve. This is the kind of question that should be asked. The agent must have assumed that it was redundant to tell Steve that Steve's present, but it makes things ugly."). The demo's frozen page draws its viewer so too, as a live server does (`Sidebar`'s `presence_of`). Agents are never online, as no sidebar lists one. When each human was last seen is kept in the server's memory, so a restart forgets it (`app/Presence.roc`, `app/PresenceNotices.roc`).
- **New activity turns the tab violet, as the Zig chat's did (Steve, 2026-11-20: "We need the browser tab to turn violet for new chat activity. Port gopher faithfully at first, but we may tweak it a bit later.").** Every message tells every other human who may read it, on the stream their conversation page or Recent page already holds: the top bar's notice line says "<name> sent you a message on <topic>." (a DM) or "<name> posted to <channel> > <topic>." (a channel), linked to the message itself (its topic's page at `#msg-N`, which opens there and selects it), and the tab's icon turns violet, unless the page is that topic's own. A came-online notice turns it violet too. It stays violet until the notice's link is followed or the notice dismissed, whether or not the tab was in view (`app/MessageNotice.roc`, `app/chrome.js`).
- **The notice line: in the bar on a desktop, a line of its own on a phone, violet for a message (Steve, 2026-11-20: "a good use of real estate on the desktop. It might not work for mobile though", "reinforce the violet theme", "status bar links should be actions"; the placement his delegated call).** A wide window keeps the line in the top bar, between the section links and the viewer's name. A phone's bar has no width to spare, so there the line is a thin row along the bar's foot, with a ✕ that dismisses the notice shown (a new one shows again), and no row when there is nothing to say. A message's notice is the tab's violet, with a violet dot; a presence notice stays the bar's muted text. Every link is the useful action: a message's opens the message, a came-online notice opens the DM with them, a new device's opens the button that signs other devices out; the DM page's drawn line of where the other human is has no link.

## Storage: the file system is the database

- **The file that makes a thing exist is written last; anything read as proof of a state is written before it.** A write of several files is never a transaction, so a crash between two writes must leave a state the rules already plan for. `name` is what makes a folder an account, so an account's other files go before it. (Steve, 2026-11-20, from the day's cold audits, each of which found a write-order gap.)
- **One folder per conversation, with its messages appended.** For our scale, the whole conversation is **one human-readable file**, readable with `cat`, author and timestamp included:

```
-------------
MSG_apoorva-autumn_13
from: Steve
date: 2026-09-03T19:11:32Z

apoorva@zulip.com ok?

-------------
MSG_apoorva-autumn_14
from: apoorva
date: 2026-09-03T19:12:28Z

apoorvavpendse@gmail.com would be better
```

- **Markdown is stored exactly as written.** A body line that is exactly the separator `-------------` is stored as `\-------------` and turned back on reading. This escaping happens at the chat's edge, outside the markdown processor. When a message looks hostile, refuse it.
- **Reactions:** one file per conversation, `reactions` beside its `transcript`, one JSON line per reaction, as the Zig chat's sidecar was, but holding only the current set, in the order the reactions were made (`{"msg":3,"uid":1,"emoji":"👍"}`), and rewritten whole, atomically, at each change (`app/Reactions.roc`). The transcript's format is unchanged: an edit is an ordinary message whose markdown starts `Edit of MSG_<topic>_<n>`.
- **Users:** a folder per user, named by the user's numeric id, holding one small human-readable file per fact, as the Zig chat keeps them. High-stakes facts, who a user is and how they prove it, live under the accounts root, `auth/`; lower-stakes ones (the upload total, later preferences) under `users/`, as the Zig chat keeps its account store under one root and each user's other state under another. Every file below holds one fact, written bare with no line end unless it says so; a reader takes ASCII spaces, tabs and line ends off both ends (`FactFile`), so a file edited by hand still reads.

  A key's or invite's number `<n>` counts 1, 2, 3 within its user's folder, and is never given twice, as nothing in an account's folder is removed, and no account is ever removed. A new human's welcome is written after the counter and before their account, so no account lacks one: a failure there spends an id and holds no name or GitHub id, and the same invite or sign-up tries again. A crash after it leaves a DM naming an id no account has, which no one can reach, as no id is given twice. An invited human's account is written after their welcome, `inviter` then `name`, then the default channel's members, and then their invite; a redemption writes the name the human chose, if it changed, then spends the invite (rewriting `state` alone), then, at a human's first join, `joined`, before it writes the password. A human who signs up with GitHub is written after their welcome, `github-id`, `joined`, then `name`, then the default channel's members, and then `github-session-secret` as their session starts. Members come after `name`, as a channel holding a member who is no user stops the chat reading its directory; a crash between the two leaves a human in no channel, whom the admin adds. A human linking GitHub has `github-session-secret` written whole, then `github-id`, so a link cut short binds no one. A record of several files is written a file at a time, each made once, `hash` last: a record cut short by a crash has no hash, so it admits no one. A revoke or a redemption rewrites `state` alone, atomically. An account is written `owner` first and `name` last, after the counter, so an id is never given twice and an agent is never read as a human. The first start writes Steve's `name`, `general.channel` holding `1`, and the counter last; a data folder holding accounts or channels and no counter is part of a chat, and the server refuses to start on it.

  The admin creates a channel at `/channels` and adds or removes its members, humans or agents, on its page, `/channels/<name>` (`app/ChannelMembers.roc`), which rewrites its `.channel` file whole, atomically; an admin may still edit the file by hand, as in the Zig chat. Removing a member never removes their account or their id. A secret never reaches the disk: a key's and an invite's are shown once and kept only as their hash, and a password only as its bcrypt hash.

  A session is not on disk: it is the cookie `__Host-gopher_auth`, whose value is the user's id in base64url (unpadded), a `.`, the second it was signed at in decimal, a `.`, and the HMAC-SHA256 under `.session-secret` of the id's digits, a line feed, that second, a line feed and what the human's sign-in files hold, in base64url. That is the hash their `password` file keeps (as its file holds it, the spaces and line ends at either end off), and, while they have a `github-id`, a line feed and the word their `github-session-secret` keeps; a human with no password has the line feed and the word alone. It is signed, never sent, so a session costs no storage and admits its human only while those files hold what it was signed over, however it began: a human with a password and GitHub has one set of sessions, and rewriting either file ends them all, as does their first GitHub sign-in, which writes `github-session-secret`, and linking GitHub, whose answer starts them a new session. It admits its human for a year from its second, and not at all when signed more than five minutes ahead of the clock; it is set `Path=/; Max-Age=31536000; HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Lax`. A session a device code started also names its device key, a fourth part `d` and the key's number (`d3`), signed over as `by-device-code 3` and a line feed after the second, and admits its human only while that key's `state` says `active`, read at every request; signing out of that device revokes the key, so every copy of its cookie ends with it. Signing out of any other session clears the browser's cookie; a copy kept elsewhere stays good until its year is out, its human's password file is rewritten or removed, or the secret is replaced. **User ids are never reused, and users are never deleted**: a session names its human by id alone, so an id given to someone new would hand them every session of the old one's. The counter only rises, and no account folder is removed.
- **Feel like REST over the file system.** A database isn't ruled out in the long run.

## Sign-in

- **GitHub sign-in, or an invite link for people without GitHub.**
- **Built now, as the Zig chat did it:** a human signs in at `/login` with their name and a password, checked against the bcrypt hash in their account folder, and gets a signed session (above). The server runs at most a burst of 5 password checks, then one a second, for everyone together; past that a sign-in is a 429 (docs/platform.md, "Passwords and the server's secret"). A wrong password, a name that is no human's, and a human with no usable hash each take one check, so the answer's time says nothing of which names exist. Steve writes his own password's hash on the server's machine, so it never passes through an agent or a log. Any human, by their own session, invites someone by a name at `/invites`: a new account holds the name at once, and the inviter is shown a link, `<origin>/join#<code>`, good once for seven days. The code follows the `#`, so no browser sends it in a URL and it reaches no log. The join page's script reads it, asks `POST /join/name` for the name the invite holds, and fills it in; the invitee keeps it or chooses any other free name, chooses a password, and joins (`POST /join`), signed in. Their name is permanent from then on, and the name they left is free. The human who invited them, or the admin, invites them again for a new link, and its redemption keeps their name and sets a new password. Joining is refused to a request with an API key, and to a browser signed in already. A request with neither a session nor an API key may only sign in, sign up by GitHub, load the pages' scripts and the site's icon, redeem an agent's invite, and join: any other page sends it to sign in, and any other request is a 401.
- **GitHub sign-in.** On a server with GitHub's settings the sign-in page leads with "Sign in or sign up with GitHub", above the password form and a line saying "Already have an account? Sign in with your password, then link GitHub." It links to `/login/github/start?next=<path>`, which sends the browser to GitHub with a state signed by the server, its nonce in a ten-minute cookie of this browser's (`__Host-gopher_github_state`). GitHub sends it back to `/login/github`, the only callback URL the OAuth app needs. The state must be signed, this browser's and under ten minutes old before the code is exchanged; the host exchanges the code with the turn let go, under a 15-second deadline, and answers GitHub's id and login. The human whose `github-id` holds that id gets a session, as a password would; two humans holding one id are refused.
- **Sign-up by GitHub is open (Steve, 2026-11-05): invites are there to reduce friction, not to keep people out.** An id no human holds is a new human. GitHub's way back sets their sign-up's cookie and sends the browser on to `GET /login/github/name`, the form that asks a name, read from that cookie, so a reload asks again. It is filled in with their GitHub login where that is a free name, and says why not when it isn't: no login this chat shows, no name here, taken, or reserved or too close to a name here (an invited human's who never joined, whose invites have all expired, counts as free). A login outside `GitHubLogin`'s rule still signs in; it is only not offered. The form says everyone sees the name, it can't be changed, and the page lasts ten minutes, and the field holds at most 60 characters. They may change the name; the rule is any other name's, and it is permanent, as an invited human's is. `POST /login/github/name` makes the account: any lapsed invitee's release in its way, the next id, the welcome, `github-id`, `joined`, `name`, then a session, which writes `github-session-secret`. A human made this way has no password and no inviter, and starts in the default channel, as an invited human does. A taken or invalid name is the form again. Between GitHub and the name the id travels in the cookie `__Host-gopher_github_sign_up`, a value signed by the server that lasts ten minutes (`app/GitHubSignUp.roc`): a browser can't rewrite it, and no other site can set it, so no one is signed in to an account someone else began. It isn't spent: once the id has an account, the same value, a second tab's, or a double submit is refused and signs no one in, and that human signs in with GitHub. A browser signed in by the time the name comes is refused too, makes no account, and links by going through GitHub again.
- **New accounts are capped** (`app/SignUpLimits.roc`, which holds both numbers): at most 20 made by GitHub sign-up in any 24 hours, server-wide, and at most 2,000 accounts in all, humans and agents, however made, an invite's included. Either is a 503, "Sign-ups are paused for now; please try again later", at GitHub's way back and again at the name, and a line in the server's log (`Host.write_line_to_server_log!`). The day's sign-ups are counted in the server's memory, twenty slots each holding its last sign-up's second, so a restart forgets them; the 2,000 still holds.
- **Abuse limits, phase 2** (Steve said yes, 2026-11-20). Each is a module of its own that holds its numbers:
  - **A new account uploads at most 100 MiB** in its first 2 days (`app/NewAccountUploads.roc`; Steve, 2026-11-20: "We can reduce the trial period to two days. I'm on it everyday. We have never been spammed yet, so our general lean is still toward reducing friction"), counted in the lifetime total; past it a 403, as the allowance's own refusal is. Its age is its `joined` file's (above, `app/JoinedFiles.roc`). A human with no `joined` file, the owner and the agents keep the lifetime allowance alone; a `joined` file that can't be read refuses the upload with a 500.
  - **30 messages a minute for each user** (`app/MessageRate.roc`), humans and agents alike, an edit counting as the message it is: past it a 429, "Too many messages: at most 30 a minute. Wait a moment, then send it again.", nothing written. A window starts at the first message after the last ended, and is kept in the server's memory, two keys a user, so a restart forgets it.

With 10^8 codes, a guesser with many addresses, whom no client's limit holds, would otherwise find a live code in reach; now they make at most 4,800 guesses a day, and with L codes live, each guess wins with probability L/10^8, so a day of guessing against one code live the whole day wins about once in 20,000 days, and a code is live only its 5 minutes.
